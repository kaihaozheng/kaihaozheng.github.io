Dear Professor,

I am applying for PhD programmes starting in Fall 2027, to work on human–water interaction. Specifically, I am interested in developing and applying quantitative methods that reduce complex human–earth interactions to their key elements, so that places can be compared and the mechanisms behind them can be taken apart.

I hope to provide scientific insights, data and frameworks for decision-makers who have to find cost-effective solutions with limited people, money and resources, and for carrying what works in one place over to others in a similar state.

Kaihao Zheng

WhatForces and elements within human–water dynamics

The forces that drive human–water systems and the conditions under which their mechanisms hold. In flood management: hydroclimate, the human footprint and settlements, and water infrastructure.

HowQuantitative methods for large-scale comparison

Frameworks that reduce complex interactions to their key elements and support comparison across many places, which is how the mechanisms behind them can be dissected.

WhyCost-effective action and knowledge transfer

With limited people, money and resources, such frameworks help decision-makers see trade-offs and synergies, and let knowledge move between places in a similar state.

Research Projects

Question
When settlements, dams and hydro-climate interact, what decides how hard flood regulation becomes?
Approach
A framework linking floodplain exposure, flood return period and dam operational load, tested first in China’s Ganjiang Basin.
Finding
Urban growth from 1985 to 2015 nearly doubled the Wan’an Dam’s load under a 100-year flood (14.1% → 26.6%). The thesis scales the framework to 4,201 urban catchments worldwide.
Paper ↗
Question
Global floodplain maps used one terrain setting everywhere, ignoring how rivers differ between regions.
Approach
Basin-specific floodplain hydraulic-geometry parameters on 90-m global terrain.
Finding
An open global map of geomorphic floodplains: 9.91 million km², 6.6% of the land surface.
Paper ↗
Question
Do levees draw cities into floodplains, and can policy change that?
Approach
Seven decades of land cover around 1,129 levee systems in the contiguous United States.
Finding
Levees went with 62% faster floodplain urban growth (29% county-wide) until the late 1970s, when the effect reversed alongside flood-insurance and floodplain-management policies.
